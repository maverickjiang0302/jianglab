Build the process

Everything is per batch. Each feed carries a mass, a dry-solids fraction and a bulk density; their volume is what the first units process. A unit takes in whatever it lists under “Receives from” and passes on what it makes: tanks and mixers pass everything on, filters keep the solids as a cake (the filtrate leaves the process), dryers remove moisture down to the outlet value, and kilns lose the mass fraction you set. Use a stream merge to combine streams and a stream split to divide one by fractions.

Per-kg results use the dry solids leaving the last units by default; for a liquid product, switch to total mass or enter your own product mass. Each unit has its own tier. Tier 1 needs no equipment size; Tiers 2–3 need Vm, the material held in the equipment, which for batch units defaults to one batch and for continuous units follows from the processing time and τ. Electricity depends only on what a unit processes; its outlet matters only downstream.

Method

Installed motor power intensity follows a power law of equipment capacity, fitted to outlier-free vendor listings (754 of 799). Electricity for each unit is

E = LF · (P/Vm) · Vb · τ / η(P)

  • Tier 1 needs no size. It resamples real units of the selected model and reports the geometric mean with the 5th–95th percentile range.
  • Tier 2 applies the fitted equation at your equipment size with the default geometry, LF = 0.60 and η from Seider et al. (Eq. 16.18) at rated power. Intervals are exact t-based 90% prediction intervals.
  • Motor efficiency at the unit’s total rated power P (in hp) follows Seider et al., Eq. 16.18: η = 0.80 + 0.0319 ln P − 0.00182 (ln P)². It is stated for 1–1,500 hp and extrapolated outside that range (Seider et al., 2016).1
  • Tier 3 lets you replace the default geometry, load factor and motor efficiency with your own values.
  • Vb is the volume processed: the inflow for tanks, mixers, dryers and kilns, and the cake volume for filters. For continuous units, Vm = (Vb / processing time) × τ.
  • Loads outside the vendor package, such as feed pumps, burners and heaters, are not included; each unit lists them.
  • Pooled filter and dryer models are not offered, because subtypes differ too much to pool.

1. Seider WD, Lewin DR, Seader JD, Widagdo S, Gani R, Ng KM. Product and Process Design Principles: Synthesis, Analysis, and Evaluation. 4th ed. Hoboken, NJ: Wiley; 2016. Eq. 16.18.
